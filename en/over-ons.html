Who you get on the line

What you have to go and fetch, you don’t do.

My background is in civil engineering, roads and waterworks. There, everything revolves around anchoring: what holds a structure in place is never the steel itself, but the point where it is fixed. Exactly the same applies to your bike.

But the real problem is somewhere else, and I notice it at home every day. You arrive with two children and your hands full. If the chain is not exactly where it belongs — at the anchor, ready to go through it — you don’t lock up. You are already busy with something else. Coat off, bag down, someone needs the toilet. And if you think of it in the evening, you are not going back outside for it.

That is how a four-thousand-euro cargo bike stands unlocked for a night. Not because you are careless, but because security that takes effort always loses at a busy moment.

That is why there should be a chain on your bike as standard, and a chain at your anchor as standard. Not one that you lug back and forth. What you have to go and fetch, you don’t do.

And at home you want the heaviest you can get. On the road you weigh things up — with a cargo bike you just throw a thick chain in the box, on an ordinary bike three kilos gets annoying. But on your wall that trade-off does not apply. It hangs there, it doesn’t need to go anywhere, so it can be as heavy as you like.

And road bikes often have no lock at all. Not even a frame lock, because that adds weight. They go indoors, until after a ride they stand against the wall for a moment while you rinse your bottles.

On top of that, hardly anyone locks to a truly fixed point. Everyone has a lock, often a good one too, but it hangs around a thirty-centimetre garden gate or a drainpipe. A thief doesn’t think twice: he lifts the whole thing, bike and lock and all. That lock is still intact when he cuts it at his leisure at home.

And then there is the box. With a new bike, scooter or motorbike an anchor is sold along with it, and it stays where it is — in the shed, behind the wheelie bin, in the hall next to the meter cupboard. Not out of unwillingness: you need to know what kind of brick you are drilling into, which fixing goes with it and how to fix a bolt so a thief cannot unscrew it. So you put it off.

That is what I come round for. I do the jobs myself, no subcontractors. If you email, you get me.

Muurslot, Sassenheim
