Your policy

A second lock is almost always required. What you lock it to varies.

This is where claims often go wrong. But first: what actually is a second lock?

Note: a bike stolen from your garden or driveway is often not covered by your home contents insurance, only by bike insurance. And that often requires a second lock, and with some insurers that you lock it to a fixed object.

Lock 1

Your frame lock

That black ring around your rear wheel that is already on your bike. You click it shut and the wheel locks. Handy against riding off — but your bike is not attached to anything, so it can simply be lifted.

Lock 2

A separate lock with its own key

A chain lock or folding lock that you use in addition, with its own lock body and its own key. With it you attach your frame to something that cannot be moved. That is the lock your insurer means.

Does not count

The plug-in chain

The chain you click into your frame lock with a pin. It shares the lock with your frame lock, so it is still one lock. None of ENRA, Nh1816 and Kingpolis accept it as a second lock. ENRA also rejects combination locks.

With every installation

A photo report for your insurer

If your bike is stolen, your insurer wants to know how it was locked. That is almost impossible to prove afterwards: a cut chain does not tell you what it was attached to. An insurance broker told us in so many words: checking whether someone used a fixed object is practically impossible.

That is why we record it at the moment the anchor goes in. With every installation we make a dated photo report: the anchor on your wall with the ART mark legible in the picture, the fixing with the steel balls in the bolt heads, and the chain through the anchor. You get it by email the same day.

If you ever make a claim, you send the report with it. It is no guarantee that your insurer will pay out, but it is solid evidence that your security was in order, date and all.

For cargo bikes

Your cargo bike can no longer be insured?

Bright reported on 26 February 2025 that ENRA no longer insures new Urban Arrow cargo bikes in twelve cities, including Amsterdam, The Hague, Leiden and Utrecht. Where it is still possible, ENRA requires a second lock of at least ART-2 for an electric cargo bike, and from €3,500 also a tracker. Other insurers have their own requirements.

But a tracker only looks for your bike once it is gone. An anchor makes sure it doesn’t get away. With us it is fixed to your own wall from €5.72 a month.

And then five things you want to know before you buy anything — from us or from someone else.

What exactly does my insurer require? Five points and a table per vehicle

Almost always: a separate second lock

ENRA, one of the largest providers behind bike insurance sold through shops, requires a second lock for every e-bike and for every bike from €3,500. For an electric cargo bike it must be at least ART-2. If it turns out after a theft that no second lock was used, there is no right to a payout.

A plug-in chain does not count

None of ENRA, Nh1816 and Kingpolis accept the chain you click into your fixed frame lock as a second lock. ENRA also rejects combination locks. You really need a separate, approved chain lock — and it has to be able to attach to something.

An anchor does not increase your payout

It makes sure you don’t need one. But it does affect your wallet: if your policy requires an anchored point and you don’t use one, expect a reduction or nothing at all. For a scooter, InShared charges a €400 excess instead of €200 if it was not locked to an object anchored in the ground.

Watch the key number

ENRA does not accept a lock whose keys have no key number, unless it comes with a key card. If you buy a chain yourself, check this. We supply our locks with a key number.

What a wall anchor is not for

Above a certain amount an activated track and trace module is required, and no anchor helps with that. With ENRA this applies to electric cargo bikes from €3,500 and to e-bikes from €4,500; ANWB and Kingpolis are also at €4,500. With Fietszeker a tracker is required for an electric cargo bike, not for a regular e-bike. Note: it must be a tracker your insurer approves. ANWB and Kingpolis require a Kiwa mark for it (ANWB also accepts the Bosch ConnectModule), and there usually has to be an active subscription — an AirTag or any GPS module does not count. With Kingpolis you are responsible for the tracker working yourself; if it did not work at the time of the theft, you will not get a replacement bike. And if your policy does not cover battery damage when the bike is outside at night, take the battery indoors. No lock helps with that either.

We are not insurance advisers, and policies really do differ. Get out your policy conditions or call your insurer, and ask three things: which ART level does my second lock need, does my bike have to be locked to a fixed object and do I need a tracker, and if so: which brands do you accept. With those answers you know exactly what you need.
